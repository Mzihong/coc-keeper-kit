# 7e Skill Checks — cheat-sheet

> Reference for setting difficulties and reading rolls. Not a rules reproduction — see the
> *Keeper Rulebook* for full text.

## The roll
Roll **1d100**, aim to roll **equal to or under** the skill/characteristic value.

## Difficulty bands
| Level | Succeed if roll ≤ | Use when |
|-------|-------------------|----------|
| **Regular** | skill value | Ordinary pressure |
| **Hard**    | skill ÷ 2 (round down) | Trained opposition, poor conditions |
| **Extreme** | skill ÷ 5 (round down) | Near the limit of possibility |
| **Critical**| 01 | Always the best possible outcome |

**Set the difficulty, then roll — don't roll then decide.** Most tasks are Regular; reserve
Hard/Extreme for genuinely harder tasks, not as a tax.

## Fumbles
- Roll **100** is always a fumble.
- If skill **< 50**, rolls **96–100** are fumbles; if skill **≥ 50**, only **100** fumbles.

## Bonus & Penalty dice
Roll one or more extra "tens" dice; take the **best** tens result for a **bonus** die,
the **worst** for a **penalty** die. They cancel one-for-one. Grant a bonus die for
favourable circumstances instead of lowering difficulty.

## Pushing a roll
After a failed **Regular** roll, the investigator may **push** — try again with more time,
effort, or risk — but a failed push triggers a real, narrated consequence. You cannot push
most combat or Sanity rolls.

## Luck
Investigators may spend **Luck points** to raise a failed roll's result up to success
(cannot turn a fumble into success; cannot buy a Critical). Luck is a finite pool that
recovers slowly — let it save the story, not trivialise it.

## Opposed rolls
Both parties roll their relevant skill. Higher **success level** wins (Extreme beats Hard
beats Regular); if tied level, higher roll-that-still-succeeds, or the defender wins ties.

## When to call for a roll
Only when **failure is interesting** and **success is uncertain**. If there's no meaningful
consequence to failure, just narrate success. Never gate the only path forward behind one
roll — see the three-clue rule in `design-scenario`.
