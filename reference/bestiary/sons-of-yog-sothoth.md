# Sons of Yog-Sothoth (犹格·索托斯之子)

*What a human union with an Outer God produces — and no two of them come out the same.*

- **Type:** servitor-race (human-Outer-God hybrid)
- **Tier:** L3 servitor — malleus labels this entry **上级仆从种族** (greater servitor race),
  and its stat block is written in dice-notation ("2D6×5", "4D6+30"), the format
  `reference/rules/monster-scale.md` treats as the L2/L3 signal (fixed values mark L4/L5
  instead). The text's "no two are alike" framing could read as unique-entity, but the source
  files it as a *race* with a rollable range, not a single named individual — that's a
  servitor-race classification, greater sub-tier, not L4.
- **Threat:** mythic — per the 上级/下级 table, a 上级仆从种族 entry takes the tier's
  `deadly`/`mythic` rows. The monstrous profile's 100% attack skill and total immunity to
  conventional weapons sit at the top of L3's mythic band even though its raw HP reads low
  (see Stat block note below).
- **Sanity to see:** 1/1D8 (sensing the unseen monstrous form) — see Stat block for the full
  spread across both profiles
- **Serves:** Yog-Sothoth
- **Index summary:** Yog-Sothoth's hybrid offspring, unseen

## Reveal (say this out loud, before the SAN roll)

At a glance it could be the neighbour's boy — gangly, big-boned, dressed a size too large for
a frame that keeps outgrowing its clothes. It's the details that catch: skin with no visible
pores, a smell like ozone and turned soil underneath the cologne, footsteps that land a
half-beat off the ground before the sound of them arrives. When it feeds, the air itself seems
to fold — bulk presses into the room from no direction you can point to, floorboards groan
under a weight nothing is standing on, and something wet and rope-thick drags itself forward
on legs that were never meant to be counted.

## Stat block (CoC 7e)

Malleus gives two very different profiles per individual — how monstrous a Son turns out is
rolled/chosen, not fixed. Most individuals sit somewhere on the spectrum between them.

| | STR | CON | SIZ | DEX | INT | POW |
|---|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| **Monstrous offspring** | 35 (2D6×5) | 35 (2D6×5) | 44 (4D6+30) | 16–17 (3D6+6) | 21 (6D6) | 21 (6D6) |
| **Human-passing offspring** | 22–23 (3D6+12) | 35 (2D6×5) | 13 (2D6+6) | 10–11 (3D6) | 21 (6D6) | 21 (6D6) |

Human-passing offspring additionally have **APP** 7 (2D6), **EDU** 13–14 (3D6+6), and
**SAN** 0 (it never had any to begin with). The monstrous profile has no APP/EDU/SAN — it
isn't a person anyone meets socially.

- **Monstrous offspring:** HP 7 · Build +5 · Damage Bonus +4D6 (malleus states this directly,
  overriding the naive STR+SIZ calculation — treat it as canon per `monster-scale.md`) ·
  Move 10
- **Human-passing offspring:** HP 4 · Build +2 · Damage Bonus +1D6 · Move 8
- The monstrous profile's HP looks low for a mythic-band entry — that's not an error. Its
  *Immunity to conventional weapons* trait (below) does the actual survivability work instead
  of a big HP pool, per `monster-scale.md`'s armour convention: prefer a narrative immunity
  over a bigger number once conventional weapons stop being a real answer.

### Combat

- **Attacks per round:** 1
- **Grasp & Drain** (monstrous profile) — skill 100%, 1D6 crushing the first round, plus
  1D10 blood-loss damage every round the grip holds after that.
- **Fist / weapon** (human-passing profile) — skill 75%, 1D3 + db, or by weapon if armed.
- **Dodge:** monstrous profile 8% (DEX/2, largely academic — it rarely needs to); human-passing
  profile 5% (DEX/2, an ordinary person's reflexes).
- **Armour:** monstrous profile — conventional weapons of any kind deal **no damage at all**;
  enchanted weapons deal only minimum damage; spells and other magical harm affect it normally
  (it is unusually sensitive to magic, inherited from its parent). Human-passing profile —
  none; it's hurt exactly like an ordinary person.

### Special abilities (traits)

- **Immunity to conventional weapons** (`reference/tables/monster-traits.md`'s 免疫常规武器) —
  the monstrous profile shrugs off anything that isn't enchanted or magical; **counter-play:**
  enchanted weapons and spells both work, and both are discoverable through the same Mythos
  research the creature itself is chasing (below). Load 2.
- **Invisible except while feeding** — native to the species, not from the trait menu (like a
  Thrall's *Transformation*, it's flavour the malleus text states outright rather than a
  bolt-on). It can be tracked by smell, sound, and disturbed ground even while unseen; it must
  become tangible — and visible — to actually feed, which is the reliable window to notice it.
- **Trait load total:** 2 / 2 (L3 servitor ceiling, per `monster-scale.md`)
- **Spells:** every individual automatically knows *Call Yog-Sothoth*, plus a number of other
  spells equal to half its INT (Keeper's choice which). Library Use 75%; otherwise skilled as
  an ordinary person of its apparent age and background.

### Sanity loss, by encounter

- Sensing the monstrous offspring while it stays unseen: **1/1D8**
- Witnessing the monstrous offspring fully revealed: **1D3/3D10**
- Meeting a human-passing offspring with only minor mutant traits: **0/1D2**
- Meeting a more visibly mutated human-passing offspring: **1/1D6**

## Behaviour & weakness

- **Wants / drive:** two hungers, both bottomless — flesh, and Mythos knowledge. It grows fast
  and needs a lot of both. It studies every tome and spell it can reach, chasing ways to help
  its Outer God parent reach further into this world; the smarter and older it gets, the more
  dangerous its resources become, not just its stat block.
- **How it's usually beaten:** never in a straight fight against the monstrous form —
  conventional weapons plain don't work on it. The fair out is finding (or already having)
  something enchanted or a working spell before the confrontation, and using the fact that it
  must reveal itself to feed: that's the one predictable moment it can be identified, cornered,
  or fled from with warning.
- **Weakness / ward:** it needs to feed regularly on flesh and blood — a pattern of local
  disappearances or animal deaths is a discoverable trail leading back to it. It also can't
  stop hunting Mythos material — denying it access to a tome or a library it's after, or
  removing that resource first, stunts its growth and its usefulness to Yog-Sothoth. Per
  malleus, no two individuals are alike, so a specific campaign should let the investigators
  find *this* one's particular ward or habit through play rather than assume the above always
  applies unmodified.

## Lore

> **KEEPER ONLY** — The literary touchstone for this entry is Wilbur Whateley and his
> monstrous twin from H. P. Lovecraft's "The Dunwich Horror": one twin raised to pass as
> human (however uncannily), the other kept hidden and fed in secret. Use that shape, not
> that story — invent your own bloodline, your own household, and your own reason a family
> agreed (or was coerced) to raise one of these. A typical origin is an isolated, generations-
> intermarried rural family that a cult identified and cultivated specifically as a host
> lineage; the human parent is often dead, absent, or complicit by the time investigators
> arrive, and rarely available as a straightforward source of exposition.
