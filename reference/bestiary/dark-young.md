# Dark Young of Shub-Niggurath (黑山羊幼仔)

*A black, barrel-bodied mass the size of a hippopotamus, tree-shaped from a distance — squat
hooved trunk below, a crown of writhing tentacles above.*

- **Type:** servitor-race
- **Tier:** L3 servitor
- **Threat:** mythic — malleus files it as 上级仆从种族 (greater servitor race); per
  `reference/rules/monster-scale.md`'s 上级/下级 guidance a greater sub-tier entry takes the
  `deadly`/`mythic` rows of the L3 band, and its HP (30–31), attack skill (80%), and full
  immunity to conventional ranged/elemental damage all land at the top of that range —
  `mythic`, not `deadly`
- **Sanity to see:** 1D3/1D10 (malleus's own figure — kept as-is)
- **Serves:** Shub-Niggurath
- **Index summary:** Shub-Niggurath's tree-tentacle avatar

## Reveal (say this out loud, before the SAN roll)

The shape at the tree line isn't a tree — no tree smells like a dug-up grave, and no tree's
trunk has just shifted its weight onto a pair of cloven hooves. What you took for a wind-stirred
canopy is dozens of rope-thick black tentacles, unknotting themselves and starting, unhurried,
to reach. Somewhere under all that bulk a mouth the width of a doorway opens, and something
green and slow drips out of it and keeps dripping long after you've decided to run.

### Combat

- **Attacks per round:** up to 4 (one swing from each of four thick "working" tentacles among
  the many that cover it — a fifth+ tentacle can restrain a grabbed victim instead — plus a
  stomp usable in place of a tentacle attack)
- **Tentacle** — skill 80%, damage Damage Bonus. A hit can instead seize the target and draw
  them toward its maw; a held victim loses 1D3 STR per round while grappled, chewed rather than
  bitten, and that STR loss does not heal naturally — it is gone for good.
- **Stomp** — skill 40%, damage 2D6 + Damage Bonus.
- **Dodge:** does not evade — it relies on reach and bulk, not agility.
- **Armour:** its body is not made of matter native to Earth. Firearms do only 1 point of
  damage (2 points if armour-piercing; shotguns always deal minimum damage regardless), and it
  takes no damage at all from fire, explosives, corrosive agents, electricity, or poison.
  Melee weapons — blades, axes, farm tools, clubs — damage it normally.

### Special abilities (traits)

- **Conventional immunity** (`reference/tables/monster-traits.md`). Guns, fire, and every
  chemical/electrical attack an investigator is likely to improvise are wasted on it; the
  counter-play the trait requires is built in and discoverable — ordinary edged or blunt melee
  weapons work exactly as they would on anything else, so a party that learns this in time can
  still stand and fight rather than only flee.
- **Trait load total:** 2 / 2 (L3 servitor ceiling)

## Behaviour & weakness

- **Wants / drive:** acts as Shub-Niggurath's proxy on Earth — it accepts the sacrifices
  offered to her, is worshipped in her place by cultists who never see the goddess herself, and
  devours those who refuse her worship. It is rare, and per its own lore it is only ever
  encountered where Shub-Niggurath is actively worshipped — it does not wander into ground with
  no cult tie to her.
- **How it's usually beaten:** at 30 HP with a permanent STR-drain grapple, a straight fight is
  a losing bet even once a party knows guns are useless. The better outs are **fled** (it will
  not leave the consecrated ground it was called to — putting distance between the party and
  that grove, field, or ritual site ends the encounter) and **tricked/warded** (it exists to
  receive sacrifices; a substitute offering already meant for the cult's rite, or disrupting the
  summoning before it completes, can keep it from manifesting at all). Only once those avenues
  are closed is **endured** — melee weapons, not firearms — the fair combat option.
- **Weakness / ward:** it is bound to the site where a cult has consecrated ground to
  Shub-Niggurath. Desecrating that ground, destroying whatever anchors the rite there (an idol,
  a ring of blood-soaked earth, a standing stone), or simply getting far enough from it removes
  the Dark Young's reason — and per its lore, its ability — to be there at all. This is
  discoverable through cult records, a survivor's account, or a Mythos tome; it is not a hidden
  ritual invented on the spot.

## Lore

> **KEEPER ONLY** — See `reference/mythos/great-old-ones/shub-niggurath.md` for the deity this
> creature answers to. A Dark Young is not an independent monster a party stumbles into; its
> presence means an active Shub-Niggurath cult is nearby and currently offering sacrifices.
> Cults that use one as an enforcer are effectively staging trial-by-monster for members who
> try to renege on a bargain struck with the Black Goat — a strong hook for a cultist NPC who
> wants out.
